Probability · Sums, conditionals, and covariance

When combining distributions and adding or integrating other variables, a surrounding distribution is obtained. If the variables are independent, then the combination can be decomposed into the product of the surrounding distributions. However, when the correlation is given as 0, it’s generally not considered independent. If X is a symmetrical distribution, and Y=X² is another distribution, then the covariance could be 0, but it’s still a dependent distribution.

The covariance is a positive semideviation. For any set of variables a, the covariance aᵀΣa represents the variance of the linear combination aᵀX. Therefore, it cannot be negative. In multivariate Gaussian distributions, the covariance 0 represents the components that are independent of each other. This is a special property of the covariance.

Review questions

When is the time when the eigen decomposition and SVD are equal?

In a semisymmetric matrix with positive eigenvalues, the eigenvalues are non-negative. Therefore, it’s possible to construct a matrix SVD using the eigenvectors. In a general matrix, the eigenvalues are always non-negative, but the eigenvalues of a semisymmetric matrix can be negative or complex numbers.

Does the direction of the training data change when the denominator of the cross-product of PCA is changed?

The difference between N and N−1 is a positive constant multiple. Therefore, their directions are the same, but their eigenvalues change proportionally. The scale of the error terms needs to be adjusted accordingly.

Which of the two losses, L1 or L2, is more sensitive to outliers?

In a large disturbance, the rate of change of L2 increases in proportion to the disturbance. The partial curvature of L1 remains constant. The changes in the coefficients of certain data, as well as the stability of the system, need to be considered along with the uniqueness of the solution.
