01 / There are two factors hidden behind those six sensors.

If there are two common causes, then the important changes can be represented in a lower-dimensional space. However, if each sensor has its own noise, then the true value will be greater than the sum of those two values.

The spread of 04 / is a type of restoration error.

In the entire set of coordinates, the sum of the squares of the coordinates is equal to the square of the observation period. When we restore the data to the top k elements, the remaining coordinates will represent the directions that were discarded. Taking the average of these values gives us the sum of the variances corresponding to the discarded directions.

Key equationX̂ = XcBkBkT
(1/N)‖Xc−X̂‖F² = Σj=k+1Dλj

The error is the average of the squared errors of all the features across all observations. The average of these errors, denoted as MSE, must be divided by D. If we use N−1 for the covariance, then the sum of the eigenvalues will also be multiplied by N−1)/N to match the current definition. It’s also important to distinguish between the error in the standardized space and the original unit error.

Xc If = UΣVᵀ is the covariance, then V(ΣᵀΣ/N)Vᵀ is the variance. The heat of V is in the direction of PCA, and σⱼ²/N represents the dispersion. It’s possible to find PCA using SVD, without having to first calculate the covariance.

Key equationXc = UΣVT,   λj = σj²/N

The truncated SVD is a method that minimizes the error of Frobenius within the given rank. The PCA method applies this concept to the centered data. If the eigenvalues are the same, then the directions within that subspace may not be unique. Even if the eigenvalues’ signs change, the projection and restoration processes remain the same.

import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(431)
latent = rng.normal(size=(180, 2))
A = np.array([[1,.1],[.8,.4],[-.4,.9],[.2,-.8],[.6,.6],[-.7,.2]])
X = latent @ A.T + .35*rng.normal(size=(180, 6))
mu = X.mean(0)
centered = X - mu
U, s, Vt = np.linalg.svd(centered, full_matrices=False)
k = 2
restored = (centered @ Vt[:k].T) @ Vt[:k] + mu
error = np.sum((X-restored)**2)/len(X)
print(error, np.sum(s[k:]**2)/len(X))

06 / The difference between compression and noise reduction

The experiment uses 180 observations and 6 channels. The results are compared by varying the number of components, the correlation between latent factors, and the level of noise. The same data are used for both scenarios, and since the data are restored using PCA, this represents within-sample compression error.

In terms of noise reduction performance, it’s necessary to have a clean signal source or independent evaluations. As the number of components increases, the observation restoration error decreases. However, it cannot be guaranteed that the signal becomes closer to a “clean” signal. It’s important to distinguish between the restoration of the observed signal and the restoration of the latent signals.

What happens if we use all the ingredients together?

After excluding the measurement errors, the observations are restored to their original state. The noise that was present in the observations is also restored.

Is the classification information for the explanation distribution 95% also present in 95%?

No. There might be information that helps to identify the targets within a small range of dispersion. The performance of subsequent tasks should be evaluated based on this information.

Is the sum of the eigenvalues discarded during training exactly equal to the independent evaluation errors?

This identity holds true for the restoration errors of the training set, where the basis is determined. Since the covariance and mean of different observations may differ, the same equation generally does not hold for independent evaluation errors.

The impact of a change of B / units in the PCA direction

Advanced B · units and standardization

Problem to explore The unit of display for channel 3 has been changed to 20 times the original unit. As a result, the first principal component has changed as well. Is there a mistake with PCA?

In the previous training observations, only the channel 3 has been changed to 20 times its original value. The information remains the same, but the unit of measurement has changed. We compare the first component of PCA, which represents the centrality of the data, with PCA, which is obtained by dividing the standard deviation of the training channel by the same value. To eliminate uncertainty in the results, we use the square of the first eigenvalue as a measure of uncertainty.

  1. Centering simply shifts the position of the elements, but it doesn’t adjust the scale. If one characteristic is increased by 20 times, the variance becomes 400 times. Also, the covariance with other characteristics also changes.
  2. In the first component that isn’t standardized, the squared loading of channel 3 is equal to 99.7%. Channels with a large numerical value tend to dominate the selection direction.
  3. After creating the average and standard deviation for training, we obtain Z=(X−μ)/s. Then, PCA is used as the appropriate value. In the reasoning process as well, the same μ,s is used. The loading values in both plots are defined in the raw space and the standardized space.
  4. Standardization isn’t always correct. The effect of scaling by unit variance varies depending on whether the signal is a precisely measured quantity, or whether it’s a noise channel that behaves almost like a constant. We need to consider both the physical meaning and the subsequent purposes of such scaling.

In a standardized space, does the minimum restoration error also mean that the square error of the original unit is also minimized?

Generally, it’s not the case. The standardized space minimizes the weighted errors, which are obtained by dividing the residual of the Cartesian coordinates by the square of the channel standard deviation. The choice of unit changes the meaning of the distance over which the optimization is performed.

Problem to explore The variance was kept at 99%. But can the classification accuracy drop to the level of a coin toss?

x₁ takes 100 equally spaced grid values from −8~8. At each x₁, include one observation for each label y=−1,+1, giving 200 observations in total. Set x₂=0.2y. x₁ varies widely but is independent of the label; x₂ varies little but determines the label completely. This is a symmetric construction without randomness.

Key equationCov(x₁,x₂)=0,   Var(x₂)=0.04
PC1 direction =[1,0]ᵀ ⇒ x̂₂=0
  1. The dispersion that is preserved by the first component is 99.8%. Since there is a significant variation in the direction of large values of x₁, it’s very good when considering only the compression indicators.
  2. In the original space, all labels are assigned using only sign(x₂). When compressed with the first component, two opposite labels can be placed within the same x₁. Therefore, any deterministic classification cannot exceed 50% in this balanced set.
  3. This 50% isn’t a result of incorrect model training. Instead, it’s a result of removing the information that would allow distinguishing between the two labels.
  4. The purpose of compression is to… X It involves the distribution and restoration of the data. In supervised learning, the number of components and the preprocessing steps need to be determined during the training process. Additionally, independent evaluations should also be conducted to ensure that the results are suitable for the classification objective.
What would happen if we changed the goal for variance explanation from 99% to 99.9% in this case?

In this configuration, the ratio of the first component is approximately 99.8166%. To satisfy the requirement of 99.9%, it is necessary to leave aside some of the second component. However, this doesn’t mean that the label information of all data can be preserved using just the general distribution threshold.
